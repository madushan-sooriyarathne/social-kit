# Source Policy

Every claim in every skill must come from an independent, verifiable third-party expert or dataset. This policy applies to the original research and to every monthly refresh. When a source is borderline, leave it out.

## Who qualifies

- **Named practitioners with a verifiable track record:** published case studies, controlled tests, client results or a long public body of work in the field (for example Jon Loomer for Meta ads, Lily Ray or Cyrus Shepard for SEO, Mike Kelley for architectural photography).
- **Independent data studies:** large datasets or experiments published by analytics vendors, agencies or research firms with the method and sample size stated (for example Socialinsider, Buffer, Motion, Haus, Ahrefs, SearchPilot, System1).
- **Peer-reviewed or academic research:** journals, working papers and university research.
- **Trade press reporting on the record:** AdExchanger, Search Engine Land, Search Engine Roundtable and similar, used for what independent people found or said, not for platform announcements.

## Excluded authors (per skill)

| Skill | Excluded |
|---|---|
| meta-ads-strategy | Anything written, published or sponsored by Meta: Meta for Business, Blueprint, newsroom, help center, Meta case studies, co-branded content, statements by Meta employees. |
| organic-growth-strategy | Same as above, covering Instagram, Facebook and Threads. |
| seo-expert | Anything authored by Google: Search Central docs and blog, Quality Rater Guidelines as a primary source, Think with Google, statements by Google employees or spokespeople (Search Liaison, Mueller, Illyes, Sullivan in his Google role), Google-sponsored studies. Third-party analysis of the 2024 Content Warehouse leak and US v. Google trial exhibits is allowed. |
| seo-copywriting | Same as seo-expert, plus Keyword Planner guidance. Google tools may be named as tools to operate, never as authorities on what ranks. |
| art-direction | Platform-authored creative guidance from Meta, TikTok, YouTube or Google, LinkedIn, Pinterest and Snapchat. Studies co-produced with a platform may be used only if flagged as such. |

## Always excluded

- Affiliate listicles, SEO content farms and AI-generated roundups
- Undated posts, anonymous authors and pages with no stated method behind their numbers
- Vendor marketing that makes claims without publishing data
- Search result snippets: a source is only cited after its own page has been read

## Grading and flags

- Use the strength labels already defined at the top of each skill's evidence file (Strong, Moderate, Weak, or Controlled, Dataset, Consensus, Single). Do not invent new labels.
- Flag vendors whose findings support a product they sell, and samples that skew to one market or business type.
- Secondhand reports of a study are Weak until the primary study is read.
- When a credible new source contradicts existing evidence, add it to the skill's disagreements section rather than silently replacing the older finding.
