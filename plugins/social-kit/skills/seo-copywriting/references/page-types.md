# Page Types

Each structure is a default. The SERP analysis wins when it shows the engine expects something else. Section names below are functions, not literal headings; write headings as natural keyword variations and questions.

## Contents
- Homepage
- Service or product page
- Location or area landing page
- Hospitality: villa, hotel or room page
- Real estate: listing or neighbourhood page
- SaaS feature or use-case page
- Comparison or alternative page
- Blog or guide (informational)

## Homepage

Primary keyword: the core category plus location or audience (for example "boutique villa in Hikkaduwa", "property management software for small landlords"). Not the brand name, which it ranks for anyway.

1. H1 with category and differentiator, one-line value proposition, primary CTA
2. What you offer, in three to five specific points with facts
3. Proof: reviews with source, numbers, recognitions
4. Links to each money page with descriptive anchors
5. How it works or what to expect
6. Short FAQ (three to five real questions)
7. Closing CTA and contact details consistent with other profiles

## Service or product page

Primary keyword: transactional or commercial ("[service] in [city]", "[product type] for [use]").

1. H1 with keyword, outcome-led subhead, CTA above the fold
2. What is included, specifics and price or price range
3. Who it suits and who it does not
4. Process or how it works (numbered)
5. Proof specific to this service
6. FAQ answering objections (cost, time, guarantees)
7. CTA
Framework: AIDA.

## Location or area landing page

Primary keyword: "[service or category] in/near [place]". Only create one per place where the brand genuinely serves or operates; near-duplicate city pages are a known losing pattern.

1. H1 with service and place
2. Local specifics: distances, landmarks, travel times, local conditions, local team or projects
3. Service details for this area
4. Local proof (reviews or projects from this area)
5. Map and contact details matching the business profile
6. FAQ specific to the place

## Hospitality: villa, hotel or room page

Primary keyword: property type plus location and differentiator ("private pool villa Hikkaduwa", "beachfront suite Galle Fort"). The evidence favours brand sites that let guests complete the task, so the page should work as a booking page, not a brochure.

1. H1 with property type, place and key feature; rate from, CTA to book or check availability
2. The stay in specifics: bedrooms, bed types, capacity, size, views, private facilities
3. Inclusions and rate details (breakfast, staff, transfers, minimum stay, seasonality)
4. Location: walking or driving minutes to beach, town, station, airport, attractions
5. Experiences nearby in the brand's own words (first-hand knowledge beats generic lists)
6. Policies: check-in, children, cancellation
7. Reviews with source
8. FAQ: best time to visit, getting there, booking direct vs OTA benefits
Schema suggestion: LodgingBusiness or HotelRoom, plus FAQ where real questions are answered.

## Real estate: listing or neighbourhood page

Listing primary keyword: property type plus place plus key feature. Neighbourhood primary keyword: "living in [area]", "[property type] for sale in [area]".

Listing:
1. H1 with type, beds, place; price; CTA to enquire or view
2. Key facts block: price, size, beds, baths, land, tenure, completion
3. Description of layout and finishes, specific not adjectival
4. Location and distances
5. Floor plan, costs (fees, taxes, service charges) with `[VERIFY]` where unknown
6. Agent contact

Neighbourhood:
1. H1 with area and intent
2. Who it suits, price ranges, property types
3. Transport, schools, amenities with distances
4. Current listings in the area
5. FAQ

## SaaS feature or use-case page

Primary keyword: "[job to be done] software", "[feature] for [audience]", "[integration] [category]".

1. H1 with the job and audience; one-line outcome; CTA (trial, demo)
2. Problem in the customer's words (APP intro)
3. How the feature solves it, with screenshots described in alt text
4. Specific outcomes, numbers with source
5. How it works (numbered steps)
6. Integrations, pricing pointer, security notes where relevant
7. FAQ answering objections
8. CTA

## Comparison or alternative page

Primary keyword: "[brand] vs [competitor]", "[competitor] alternatives". Write only where the brand is a genuine alternative, and be fair; one-sided pages and mass-produced "X vs Y" pages are a known losing pattern.

1. H1 with both names; one-paragraph verdict on who each suits
2. Comparison table with verifiable facts (price, features, limits) and a date
3. Where the competitor is better, stated honestly
4. Where the brand is better, with proof
5. Migration or switching path
6. FAQ

## Blog or guide (informational)

Primary keyword: a question or "how to" with clear fit to the brand (business value 2 or 3). The job is authority, AI citation and a path to a money page.

1. H1 with the question or topic
2. Direct answer in the first 40 to 60 words
3. Sections for each fan-out sub-question, each opening with a short answer
4. Original input: brand data, first-hand experience, photos, expert quotes
5. Natural link to the relevant money page where the reader's next step is the brand's offer
6. Short summary or checklist
Framework: APP intro, then answer-first sections.
