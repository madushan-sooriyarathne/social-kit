# Keyword Research Process

The goal is one defensible primary keyword per page, with the secondaries and questions that page must cover. You usually work without paid tool access, so the process has two tracks: use the user's exports when they exist, otherwise infer demand from live SERPs and label it honestly.

## Step 1: Seeds (five sources, from Ahrefs' method)

1. How customers describe the offer: reviews, enquiries, Reddit, Quora, TripAdvisor, G2, forums. Backlinko recommends mining these for real phrasing.
2. Competitors' navigation labels and page names.
3. Competitors' ranking pages (visible by searching the category in the target market).
4. The brand's own best pages, ads and brochures.
5. A plain description of the business turned into the questions a buyer would ask.

Add the location, audience and modifiers that fit the market: "[category] in [area]", "best", "near [landmark]", "for families", "price", "vs", "how to".

## Step 2: Expand

- **With an export** (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer "Matching terms", Semrush Keyword Magic Tool, Search Console queries): filter KD at or below the site's Domain Rating, sort by Traffic Potential, check "Lowest DR" in the top 10, exclude terms the site already ranks well for, check trend and seasonality.
- **Without an export:** run searches for each seed in the target market. Harvest related searches, People Also Ask questions, phrasing in the titles of ranking pages, and the subtopics competitors build pages around. Search Console data from the user is the most reliable free source; ask for it when the site already exists.

## Step 3: Read difficulty correctly

KD scores reflect links to the top 10, not content quality or intent fit. Without a score, judge difficulty from the SERP itself:

- **Hard:** dominated by major aggregators, national brands or government and institutional sites, all closely matching the intent.
- **Winnable:** mixed results, forums, thin or outdated pages, sites of similar size to the brand, or local results where the brand has a physical presence.

## Step 4: Classify intent from the live SERP

Label each candidate informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational, and flag local or branded. Then note the three Cs of the top results: content type (product, category, service, listing, guide, tool), format (list, comparison, how-to, landing page) and angle (budget, luxury, for beginners, current year). Mixed SERP: target the dominant intent or plan separate pages.

## Step 5: Score business value (0 to 3)

- 3: the brand's product is the obvious answer; the searcher is ready to act.
- 2: the product is a strong option among several.
- 1: the topic relates but the product is a weak fit.
- 0: no fit.

Weight commercial and transactional intent higher when the goal is conversions. Informational question keywords increasingly lose clicks to AI Overviews (Ahrefs measured a 34.5% to 58% drop in position-1 CTR), so use them mainly for authority, AI citation and internal links to money pages.

## Step 6: Cluster into pages

Keywords whose top results are largely the same pages belong on one page (the Parent Topic idea). Without a tool, compare the top 5 URLs for two keywords: if three or more overlap, treat them as one page. If the broader parent term has more demand, make it the primary.

Then arrange pages as hubs and spokes: a hub page for the core topic, spoke pages for distinct intents, linked with descriptive anchors.

## Step 7: Gap check

List what competitors rank for that the brand has no page for. With Ahrefs or Semrush this is Competitive Analysis or Keyword Gap; without, compare competitor site structures against the brand's.

## Step 8: Prioritize

Order pages by business value first, then by demand relative to difficulty. Flag pages where an AI Overview dominates the SERP.

## Step 9: Keyword map (output)

| Page | Primary keyword | Secondaries | Questions to answer | Intent | SERP format | Demand signal | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Demand signal:** a number only if it comes from the user's export (name the tool). Otherwise High, Medium or Low with the reason ("4 aggregators and an AI Overview target it", "only forum results").
- **Confidence:** how sure you are the page can rank and convert for this term, with one line of why.

Never present an inferred demand level as a volume figure.
