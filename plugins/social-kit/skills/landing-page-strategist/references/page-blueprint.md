# Page Blueprint

Build the page from the visitor's arriving promise outward. Every section exists to answer a question or remove an objection that discovery found; if a section answers nothing, cut it. Design mobile first, because most hospitality, real estate and paid social traffic arrives on phones.

## 1. Message match

For each traffic source, write the promise the visitor arrives with, then make the hero restate it in the same words.

| Source | Promise in the ad, email or post | Hero headline must say | Hero image must show |
|---|---|---|---|

If sources promise different things (a Meta ad selling a honeymoon, a search ad selling a 4-bedroom pool villa), give each its own page variant or a source-specific hero. One generic page for all sources is the most common message match failure. (Gardner, Ash, CXL; Moderate.)

## 2. Awareness level and page length

| Awareness (Schwartz, via Wiebe) | Typical traffic | Page shape |
|---|---|---|
| Most aware | Branded search, retargeting, past guests, email to list | Short: offer, price or availability, proof, CTA |
| Product aware | Comparison searches, OTA spillover, referrals | Offer, differentiators vs alternatives, proof, FAQ, CTA |
| Solution aware | Category search ("private pool villa Galle", "property management software") | Outcome, how it works, specifics, proof, objections, CTA |
| Problem aware or unaware | Meta and TikTok prospecting, display | Lead with the outcome or feeling, build desire, then specifics and proof, then CTA |

Length is set by the objections that need answering, not by a word count.

## 3. The hero (first screen)

Attention is front-loaded (NN/g: about 57% of viewing time above the fold) but people scroll when the first screen signals more.
- **Headline:** the arriving promise, specific and in customer language. "Private 4-bedroom pool villa, 8 minutes from Unawatuna beach" beats "Luxury redefined".
- **Subhead:** the main differentiator or who it is for, with one hard fact.
- **Primary CTA:** a verb tied to the next step ("Check availability", "Book a viewing", "Start free trial"), visible without scrolling on a 375 px wide phone.
- **Proof point:** the strongest single proof (rating with source and count, a recognisable press mention, a client logo set).
- **Image:** the real product or place carrying information (the pool, the view, the dashboard), not decoration. NN/g eyetracking: decorative images are ignored.
- **Let the next section peek** above the fold to avoid the illusion of completeness from a full-screen hero.

## 4. Section library

Choose and order from these by awareness level and discovery findings:

| Section | Answers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Offer specifics | What exactly do I get, at what price? | Prices, inclusions, capacity, terms as text, not inside images |
| How it works | What happens after I click? | Numbered, 3 to 5 steps; reduces anxiety |
| Differentiators | Why this and not the alternative? | The competitive gap from discovery, stated as facts |
| Proof | Can I trust this? | Reviews with source, numbers, press, logos, case results |
| Visual evidence | What will it look like? | Gallery or screenshots that answer buyer questions |
| Objections and FAQ | What could go wrong? | From reviews, enquiries and sales notes; answer directly |
| Risk reversal | What if it does not work out? | Cancellation, guarantee, free trial terms, stated precisely |
| Closing CTA | Ready? | Repeat the primary CTA with the form or link |

## 5. CTAs

- One conversion goal per page (attention ratio close to 1:1; Gardner, Weak to Moderate). Remove global navigation on paid campaign pages where the brand allows.
- A secondary CTA is fine when it serves the same goal through another channel (WhatsApp, phone, calendar). Track each separately.
- Repeat the primary CTA after major sections on long pages, with the same wording.
- Make it visible through contrast with surroundings, not a particular colour; button hue tests are a known myth.
- The CTA label states the action and the outcome; avoid "Submit".

## 6. Proof and trust

- Put proof next to the decision point (beside the CTA and form), not in a distant section.
- Specific beats generic: "4.9 on Booking.com from 212 reviews" with the source; a named guest or client with a detail.
- Never invent ratings, counts, quotes or logos. Mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Irrelevant or fake-looking proof backfires (stock "customers", unverifiable badges).
- Security and privacy cues near forms and payment steps.

## 7. Copy rules

- Use voice-of-customer phrasing (Copyhackers). Reviews and enquiries supply headline and FAQ wording.
- Lead with the outcome for hedonic purchases (Talia Wolf's emotional targeting: privacy, ease, the feeling of the stay), then back it with facts. For utilitarian and B2B purchases, lead with the job done and the evidence.
- Specifics over adjectives. Cut filler ("nestled", "unparalleled", "world-class").
- One recommended headline per page; add one alternative only when it is a planned test.
- Hand full copy production to the seo-copywriting skill when the page also needs to rank.

## 8. Visual direction from brand materials

From the guidelines, logo, colours, fonts and photography, record:
- Brand tokens the build must use (colours, type scale, spacing) and the CTA treatment that contrasts within the palette
- Which supplied photos carry information for each section, and which sections lack usable imagery (route to the art-direction skill for a shoot plan)
- Voice rules and words to avoid
- Any brand rule that conflicts with conversion (low-contrast CTA colour, mandatory full-screen video hero) and the compromise proposed

## 9. Mobile and accessibility

- Tap targets large enough for a thumb; sticky CTA bar on mobile that never covers form fields or content.
- `tel:` and `https://wa.me/<number>` links for call and WhatsApp.
- Correct input types and autocomplete (see forms.md).
- Visible labels, sufficient contrast, logical focus order, errors announced to screen readers, nothing conveyed by colour alone. Accessibility is a requirement, not a test idea; it overlaps with conversion (NN/g: placeholder-only labels hurt users with impairments most).

## 10. Blueprint format

One row per section, top to bottom:

| # | Section | Purpose (question or objection) | Content from brand materials | Proof | CTA | Evidence note | [VERIFY] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Follow the table with the hero copy (headline, subhead, CTA label, proof line) written out in full.
