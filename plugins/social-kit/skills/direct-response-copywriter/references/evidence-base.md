# Evidence Base for Direct Response Copy (independent sources only, current to November 2026)

Every rule in this skill should trace back to something here or to a newer independent finding from the freshness check. Strength labels: **Controlled** = randomized experiment, A/B test with reported method, or peer-reviewed field study; **Dataset** = large benchmark or correlational dataset; **Consensus** = shared by several named practitioners, not experimentally established; **Single** = one named expert's framework or view.

**[Vendor]** marks data from firms that sell the tool being measured (ESPs, landing page builders, ad management vendors, research platforms). Their samples are their own customers and the data is not independently audited. Nothing here is authored by Google, Bing, Microsoft Advertising or any other search engine.

## 2. Reading, scanning and readability

- **Nielsen Norman Group (Jakob Nielsen, 1997):** 79% of test users always scanned new pages; 16% read word by word. Concise text improved measured usability 58%, scannable layout 47%, objective (non-promotional) language 27%, all three combined 124%. **Controlled**, dated, small samples, measured usability not conversion. NN/g also reports newsletters are read even more abruptly than web pages.
- **Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report 2024** (41,000 pages, 464M visits, 57M conversions, July 2023 to July 2024): pages at a 5th to 7th grade reading level converted at an 11.1% median, 8th to 9th grade 7.1%, professional level 5.3%. In SaaS the gap was 12.9% vs 2.1%. **Dataset [Vendor]**, correlational: simpler pages may also carry simpler offers.
- Implication: short sentences, one idea per paragraph, meaningful subheads, bullets, and no marketese.

## 3. Headlines, appeals and specificity

- **John Caples, Tested Advertising Methods:** built on mail-order split runs where coupons measured response. Headlines appealing to self-interest or giving news worked best; long headlines that say something beat short headlines that say nothing; specifics are more believable than generalities. Caples reported one ad selling 19.5 times as much as another for the same product. **Controlled (historical)**, print era, self-reported. Treat the principles as durable and the numbers as illustrative.
- Copy frameworks (PAS, AIDA, BAB, 4 U's from the AWAI and Michael Masterson tradition) are drafting scaffolds. No comparative evidence shows one outperforms another. **Consensus.**

## 4. Messaging foundation

- **Eugene Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising (1966):** five awareness stages (Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware); the less aware the reader, the more indirect the opening. Five sophistication stages, from first-to-market plain claims to a market that believes nothing and must be reached through identification. Copy channels existing desire; it cannot create it. **Consensus** (foundational, never formally tested as a model).
- **April Dunford, Obviously Awesome:** competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value and proof, target market characteristics, market category, relevant trends, in that order. "Unique" only means something relative to the alternatives. **Single** (based on work with 200+ technology companies; widely adopted in B2B and SaaS).
- **Voice of customer (Joanna Wiebe, Copyhackers; Joel Klettke):** mine reviews of what the audience already buys, skip extreme ratings for the descriptive middle, copy exact phrases, sort into before state and dream state. Thank-you page surveys capture objections from real converters. **Consensus** for the method; not validated against non-VoC copy in a controlled study.
- **Jobs to be done:** frame the purchase as progress in a situation, drawn from VoC. **Consensus.**
- **Wynter message testing:** Clarity, Relevance, Value, Differentiation, plus Friction; panel of 80,000+ B2B professionals. **Single [Vendor]**, B2B only.

## 5. Message match

The landing page headline should restate the ad's promise in the same words with the same offer. **Consensus** (Oli Gardner, Wiebe, CXL). No independent controlled dataset quantifying the lift was found; present it as strong practitioner consensus.

## 6. Landing page benchmarks, length and friction

Unbounce 2024 medians **[Vendor]**:

| Segment | Median | Top quartile |
|---|---|---|
| All industries | 6.6% | varies by industry |
| Travel and hospitality | 4.8% | 15.6% |
| Transportation and travel services | 14.8% | not captured |
| SaaS | 3.8% | 11.6% |
| Ecommerce | 4.2% | 11.4% |
| Financial services | 8.3% | 26.1% |
| Real estate | not verified in primary data | secondary figures near 3.5%, use with caution |

- Email-sourced ecommerce traffic converted at a 28.6% median (Unbounce, secondhand). **Dataset [Vendor].**
- Length: Unbounce found SaaS pages of 250 to 725 words and finance pages of 195 to 715 words converted best in its data. Classic DR (Caples, Ogilvy) and modern CRO agree length should fit awareness, price and risk. **Consensus plus Dataset [Vendor].**
- **Baymard Institute** (independent research firm): 70.19% average cart abandonment; 42% were just browsing; 40% of US shoppers abandoned because extra costs were too high; guest checkout is often buried; Baymard estimates the average ecommerce site could lift conversion 35% through checkout design alone (their estimate, not a measured average). **Dataset.**
- CTA labels that name the outcome ("Check availability") over the action ("Submit"). **Consensus.**

## 7. Email: flows, design, personalization, abandonment

- **Klaviyo 2026** (200,000+ brands, ecommerce-skewed) **[Vendor]**: flows 5.58% click, 2.11% placed order vs campaigns 1.69% and 0.16%. Welcome series 6.5% click (top 10%: 13.3%), $5.75 revenue per recipient (top 10%: $13.27). Abandoned cart highest RPR of any flow at $6.77 (top 10%: $13.70), 6% click (top 10%: 11.3%). Browse abandonment $2.08 RPR, 5.6% click. Win-back lowest at $0.82 RPR. Flows win partly through selection (they trigger on intent), not copy alone.
- Abandoned cart timing: Klaviyo suggests a reminder after 2 to 4 hours, then about 24 and 48 hours. **Vendor guidance, not test results.** HubSpot quotes Omnisend: merchants sending one cart email got 14.76 orders vs 24.94 with three. The underlying Omnisend study was not found; verify before quoting externally.
- **Val Geisler, Dinner Party Strategy:** Welcome, Appetizer, Main Course, Sides, Dessert, Invite Back; avoid "Welcome to [brand]" subject lines; widen gaps between emails. **Single**, built for SaaS onboarding.
- **HubSpot A/B tests** (article updated June 13, 2025; tests about 2014, B2B list) **[Vendor]**: the simpler design won every test. Plain text got 42% more clicks than a GIF version; an HTML template with images got 51% fewer clicks; simple HTML beat heavy HTML by 30% more clicks. Deliverability was equal.
- **Sahni, Wheeler and Chintagunta (Marketing Science, 2018):** randomized field experiments, millions of emails, three companies. Adding the recipient's name lifted opens 9.05% to 10.80%, leads 31% (0.39% to 0.51%), cut unsubscribes 17%. **Controlled.**
- **Defau and Zauner (Marketing Letters, 2023):** no first-name effect; title plus surname helped slightly. **Controlled.** Working rule: personalize on behaviour and context.
- Subject line length: published tests conflict. **Contested**, no rule.
- Benchmarks **[Vendor]**: Mailchimp (December 2023) 35.63% open, 2.62% click; Klaviyo 2026 campaigns 31% open, 1.69% click; flows 32.2% open, 5.58% click; Omnisend 2026 abandoned cart 37.12% open, 4.13% click, 1.72% conversion.
- Laura Belgray, Chase Dimond, Copyhackers: one job and one CTA per email; real deadlines are legitimate urgency, fake ones erode trust. **Consensus.**

## 8. Open rates and Apple Mail Privacy Protection

- MPP (September 2021, iOS 15) pre-fetches tracking pixels, recording an open whether or not anyone reads the email, and hides open time and location.
- Litmus: over 50% of opens happen on MPP devices; Litmus's July 2026 Email Client Market Share Report (over 1 billion opens) put Apple at 62.26% of tracked opens, Gmail 27.03%, up from 45.51% Apple in February 2026. **Dataset [Vendor].**
- Consequences: open rates inflated, CTOR deflated (inflated denominator), send-time optimization and open-based automations and inactivity rules broken.
- Corporate security scanners can auto-click links, inflating B2B click rates. **Consensus** among deliverability practitioners.

## 10. Testing statistics

- **Evan Miller, "How Not To Run an A/B Test" (2010):** peeking 10 times turns an apparent 1% significance level into a real 5%; stopping at the first 5% reading in his example produced a 26.1% false positive rate. Rule of thumb for sample size per variant: n = 16 × p × (1 − p) ÷ δ², p = baseline rate, δ = absolute difference to detect. **Controlled (statistics).**
- **Alex Molas (October 2025):** Bayesian A/B testing with a fixed posterior threshold also inflates false positives under peeking. **Single** (simulation).
- Sample ratio mismatch: if the observed split differs significantly from the design (chi-square), the test is broken. Standard practice; stated here without relying on search-engine-authored sources.

## 11. Response vs brand

**Binet and Field** (IPA Databank, 996 campaigns): best combined short and long-term profit at about 60% brand building and 40% activation for B2C. For B2B ("The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing", 2019) about 46% brand and 54% activation. Emotional campaigns produced more long-term business effects than rational ones. **Dataset**, awards-entry sample. The B2B report was published by the LinkedIn B2B Institute (Microsoft-owned), so treat the publisher as commercially interested even though the analysis is Binet and Field's. Implication: response metrics capture activation only, so DR copy must not cheapen the brand voice, especially for luxury and premium categories.

## 12. Myths and weak claims

| Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| There is a universal best send time | Myth | Vendor studies conflict; MPP hides real open times. Test on your own list using clicks |
| Power words always lift response | Weak | No controlled evidence for universal lists; Caples' durable principles are self-interest, news and specificity |
| Button colour X converts best | Myth | Single-site tests don't generalize; contrast and a clear label matter more than hue |
| Always put the first name in the subject line | Contested | 2018 positive, 2023 failed replication |
| Short copy always wins, or long copy always wins | Myth | Length fits awareness, price and risk |
| High open rate means a healthy program | Myth since 2021 | MPP inflates opens |
| High CTR means a winning ad | Weak | CTR can rise while conversion quality falls |
| A good conversion rate is 10% everywhere | Misleading | Medians range from 3.8% (SaaS) to 12.3% (entertainment) in Unbounce data |
| Fake scarcity and countdowns are harmless tactics | Excluded on principle | Not an evidence question: invented urgency misleads buyers and breaches consumer protection rules in many markets. Real deadlines are fine (Consensus: Belgray, Dimond, Copyhackers) |

## 13. Expert disagreements and working rules

| Topic | One side | Other side | Working rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long vs short copy | Caples, Ogilvy favour long copy | NN/g and modern CRO favour concise text | Long enough to answer every objection for that awareness level, written for scanners |
| Plain text vs designed email | HubSpot tests favour simplicity | Ecommerce and hospitality brands rely on imagery | Default to light design; test heavier imagery only where the product is visual |
| Name personalization | Sahni et al. positive | Defau and Zauner no first-name effect | Personalize on behaviour and context |
| Brand vs response | Binet and Field warn about short-termism | DR optimizes response | Keep brand voice fixed in the hierarchy; test angles and offers within it |
| Frameworks | Widely taught | No comparative evidence | Drafting checklists only; let tests decide |
| Benchmarks | Vendors differ by 5+ points on open rates | | Benchmark against your own history first, industry medians second |

## 14. Caveats and known gaps

- Most quantitative benchmarks are vendor data skewed to each vendor's users (Klaviyo and Omnisend to ecommerce, Mailchimp to small businesses, Unbounce to businesses that build dedicated landing pages).
- The strongest controlled evidence is dated (NN/g 1997, Caples print era, HubSpot about 2014) or contested (name personalization). Quote principles, not exact percentages, as current.
- Message match, VoC, awareness levels and frameworks rest on practitioner consensus.
- Not yet covered with fresh evidence: GoodUI pattern data, Talia Wolf's emotional targeting, Harry Dry's examples, independent controlled studies on form field counts. Candidates for the next refresh.
