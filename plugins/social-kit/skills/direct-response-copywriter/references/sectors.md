## Luxury hospitality and travel

- **North-star metric:** direct bookings and direct-booking share vs OTAs; enquiries via WhatsApp or email where that's how bookings arrive.
- **Numbers** [Vendor]: travel search CTR 9.32%, conversion 5.83%, cost per lead $44.70 (WordStream/LocaliQ); travel and hospitality landing page median 4.8%, top quartile 15.6% (Unbounce). High CTR with lower page conversion means the gap is on the page.
- **Sophistication:** usually stage 4 to 5. "Luxury", "tranquil" and "nestled" are exhausted. Win with identity (who this stay is for) and specific sensory proof (what you hear at 7 am, minutes to the beach, the chef's name `[VERIFY]`).
- **VoC sources:** TripAdvisor, Booking, Airbnb and Trustpilot reviews of the property and its competitors; enquiry threads.
- **Common objections:** price vs hotels, location and transfer, privacy, cancellation, what's included, whether it matches the photos.
- **Copy rules:** show rate or "from" price and what's included early; state the cancellation policy near the CTA; explain why booking direct is better (only real benefits); prepare visitors for the booking engine hand-off. Low reading level with sensory specifics. Protect the brand voice (Binet and Field).
- **Emails:** booking abandonment (dates and villa, availability only if true), pre-arrival (reduce anxiety), post-stay (review request, return-stay offer), seasonal campaigns to past guests.
- **Source markets:** write for where guests come from (UK, Germany, Australia, India and others): spelling, currency, travel time framing.

## Real estate

- **North-star metric:** qualified enquiries and booked viewings, not raw leads.
- **Numbers** [Vendor]: search CTR 7.61% but conversion 3.70% and cost per lead $102.51; Facebook lead campaigns convert at 9.53% (WordStream/LocaliQ). Cheap clicks, poor conversion: ad copy should pre-qualify on location, price band and timeline.
- **Sophistication:** stage 3 to 5. Mechanism (payment plan, rental management, title clarity) and proof (completed projects, rental yields with source) beat adjectives.
- **Copy rules:** key facts block (price, size, beds, tenure, completion, fees) as text; honest location and distances; claims about returns or yields only with a source and flagged for compliance review; the CTA asks for the viewing or the brochure, with what happens next.
- **Emails:** saved-search and viewed-listing triggers, new-listing alerts, project progress updates for off-plan buyers, post-viewing follow-up.

## SaaS

- **North-star metric:** trials or demos that activate, then paid conversion and pipeline.
- **Numbers** [Vendor]: lowest Unbounce median at 3.8%, but the strongest readability effect (12.9% at grade 5 to 7 vs 2.1% at professional level) and best performance at 250 to 725 words.
- **Sophistication:** stage 3 to 4. Lead with the mechanism or the category frame (Dunford), not a bare claim like "all-in-one".
- **Validation:** Wynter-style message testing for B2B; 5-second tests on the hero.
- **Copy rules:** the job and the audience in the headline; outcomes with numbers and sources; honest comparison with the real alternative (often spreadsheets or doing nothing); pricing pointer; objections on security, setup effort and switching.
- **Emails:** Geisler's Dinner Party onboarding tied to activation milestones; trial-ending sequence; feature adoption nurture.

## Ecommerce

- **North-star metric:** revenue per visitor and per recipient, conversion rate, average order value.
- **Evidence:** Baymard: 70.19% average cart abandonment, 40% over extra costs; checkout design could lift conversion 35% by Baymard's estimate. Klaviyo: abandoned cart flows earn the most revenue per recipient.
- **Copy rules:** shipping, returns and total cost stated before checkout; product pages that answer fit, use and care questions from reviews; real scarcity only.

## Local services

- **North-star metric:** calls, WhatsApp clicks, booked appointments.
- **Copy rules:** service plus area in the headline; proof from local customers; hours, coverage and response time; click-to-call and WhatsApp as primary CTAs where that's how leads arrive.

## Any other sector

1. Start from VoC, not from the brand's own description of itself.
2. Diagnose awareness and sophistication before choosing an angle.
3. One page, one audience, one action.
4. Specific proof beside every claim.
5. Judge on clicks and conversions, sized before launch.
