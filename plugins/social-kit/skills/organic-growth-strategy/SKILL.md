# Organic Growth Strategy (Meta platforms)

Turn a brand brief into a practical organic growth strategy for Instagram, Facebook and Threads, grounded in independent benchmark data rather than platform marketing.

The evidence base lives in `references/evidence.md` (benchmarks, effect sizes, disagreements between studies). Read it before building the plan, because every recommendation in the output should trace back to it. The deliverable structure lives in `references/output-template.md`.

## Source policy

The strategy relies on independent third-party data only: large-sample benchmark studies (Socialinsider, Buffer, Metricool, Emplifi, Dash Social, Sprout Social, Quid/Rival IQ, Virev), peer-reviewed research and credible independent practitioners. Leave out anything published by Meta or said by Meta executives, and give low weight to articles that only repeat Meta guidance without their own data. The reason is that platform guidance is promotional and often untested, while third-party data shows what actually moved reach.

## Workflow

### 1. Gather the brief

Work out these inputs. Research what you can before asking, and ask only for what research cannot reveal.

- Brand name, website, and social handles
- Industry and what they sell
- Audience: who, where, and which time zone they are in
- Primary goal: awareness, follower growth, leads/bookings, or sales
- Current state per platform: follower counts, posting frequency, which formats they use (if known)
- Capacity: realistic posts per week, whether they can shoot video, whether they have photo or UGC assets
- Creator or partner options: local creators, complementary brands, budget for collabs

If the user gives only a brand name or URL, research first, then ask one short round of questions covering capacity and goal, since those decide the cadence and the format mix. If the user is unavailable, assume a small team producing 3-5 posts a week with phone-shot video, state that assumption at the top, and continue.

### 2. Research the brand

- Read the brand's website to understand the offer, visual assets, tone and differentiators.
- Look at their Instagram, Facebook and Threads presence. Meta pages often block automated fetching, so if you cannot read them, say so and ask for follower counts or screenshots rather than guessing numbers.
- Identify 2-3 competitors or comparable accounts in the same niche and location, and note what formats and themes they lean on.

### 3. Diagnose

- Place each account in its follower-size tier (1-5K, 5-10K, 10-50K, 50-100K, 100K-1M, 1M+). The tier changes the right format mix, so this step matters more than it looks.
- Check the industry notes in the evidence file (for example, Reels clearly win in hospitality, restaurants and travel, but have no engagement edge in e-commerce and jewelry).
- Compare current behaviour to the benchmarks: posting frequency, single-image share, link posts on Facebook, hashtag reliance, reply habits, collab use, reposted content.
- List the 3-5 biggest gaps, ranked by expected impact.

### 4. Build the strategy

Apply the evidence, adjusted for this brand:

- **Platform roles.** Instagram is the growth engine by default. Facebook is a maintenance channel unless the audience skews strongly to Facebook (common for older or local audiences). Threads is opportunistic. Shift these roles only with a stated reason.
- **Format mix by tier.** Under 50K followers, lean Reels-first (about 60% Reels, 40% carousels). At 50K and above, weight carousels equally or more. Keep single images under 15% of feed posts.
- **Cadence within capacity.** Target 3-5 Instagram feed posts a week, rising to 6-9 only if quality holds. A plan the team cannot sustain is worse than a modest one, because silent weeks carry a measurable penalty.
- **Content pillars.** Define 3-5 pillars tied to the brand's offer and audience, each mapped to the format that suits it (Reels for discovery and shareable moments, carousels for saveable guides, Stories for conversation and retention).
- **Growth levers.** Include a collab plan (creator-published Collab posts with partners of equal or larger reach), a reply routine for the first 24-72 hours after posting, and keyword-rich captions instead of hashtag stacks.
- **Concrete ideas.** Write specific first-month post ideas for this brand, not generic templates. Each idea names the format, the hook for the first frame or slide, and the pillar it serves.

### 5. Write the deliverable

Follow `references/output-template.md`. Keep the writing concise and direct, and tie key recommendations to their evidence with short inline references such as "(Buffer, 2.1M posts)". Deliver it as a document the user can keep and share, using whatever document output the current environment favours, or a Markdown file if nothing better is available.

## Keeping the evidence current

The evidence file reflects independent data available as of November 2026. If today's date is more than about six months later, run a quick search for the newest Socialinsider, Buffer and Metricool benchmark releases before building the plan, apply the same source policy, and note in the deliverable which figures were refreshed. Platform rules also change (the April 2026 originality change to recommendations is one example), so check for major policy changes reported by independent outlets.

## Honesty about the evidence

Most effects in the data are correlations from tool vendors' customer bases, and different studies use different denominators (followers, reach or views), which is why their headline numbers can conflict. State that briefly in the deliverable's caveats. Areas with thin evidence (Lives, Facebook Groups, broadcast channels, Story stickers) should be framed as tests to run, not proven tactics.
