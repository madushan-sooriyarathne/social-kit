## Ecommerce / DTC

The playbook applies most directly here because most of the evidence comes from this vertical.
- Purchase with value, Sales objective, value optimization once volume allows.
- Catalog / Advantage+ catalog ads when the product range is large.
- CTC's four-campaign structure (Evergreen vs Marketing Moment × Acquisition vs Retention) once spend supports it.
- Expect offer-led formats to look strongest around sales periods; Motion's data window included BFCM.

## Luxury hospitality, hotels and villas

- **Track the booking engine.** Pixel and CAPI on the booking steps so a completed reservation is a purchase with its value. If the booking engine is third-party, confirm it supports the pixel and CAPI or server-side events; this is often the main blocker.
- **Benchmark against OTAs.** Judge results on direct revenue minus ad cost against the 15 to 25% commission OTAs (Booking.com, Expedia) charge per completed booking (RateGain, 2026). A direct booking at a 12% ad cost is a win even when ROAS looks modest.
- **Meta's role.** Hospitality practitioners position Meta as strongest for inspiration and bringing warm audiences back, with Google Hotel Ads, metasearch and Search capturing active booking intent (Hostfully, Mediaboom). Cold-traffic social ads are the hardest to make work in this vertical (Hostfully). Recommend Meta alongside search capture, not instead of it.
- **Catalogs.** Dynamic travel / catalog ads suit hotel groups with changing inventory. A single villa gains little from a catalog.
- **Click-to-WhatsApp.** For villas, group stays, weddings and retreats, guests often want to ask before booking, so click-to-WhatsApp can beat a landing page. Only recommend it if someone will reply within minutes.
- **Volume.** Bookings are few and high-ticket, so the account may never exit learning on a purchase event. Consolidate into one ad set, keep the booking event if results can be judged over 4+ week windows, and use enquiries as a secondary signal.
- **Creative angles that fit.** The stay as an experience (morning, dining, the view), guest testimonials, host or founder-led video, offer-first statics for shoulder seasons, simple text-led "letter" style posts. Lo-fi phone footage of the property often beats the glossy shoot.
- **Unverified budget claims:** Scale Growth Digital claims boutique hotels can shift 10 to 20% of bookings to direct on $1,000 to $3,000/month; hostAI suggests ad spend of 5 to 10% of the direct-booking revenue goal. Use as rough sanity checks only.

## Real estate

- **Housing Special Ad Category** is mandatory when advertising from or targeting the US, Canada, the UK, the EU and several other European countries (Aurelius Media). It removes age, gender, ZIP-code and most detailed targeting; US ads need a 15-mile minimum radius. It applies based on the audience, so a Sri Lankan developer targeting diaspora buyers in the UK or EU is bound by it.
- Creative and qualifying form questions do the filtering. Special Ad Audiences built from closed clients replace standard lookalikes.
- Optimize toward booked viewings or qualified CRM stages via CAPI rather than raw form fills; raw lead volume in real estate is notoriously low quality.
- Speed-to-lead matters heavily; recommend a response process alongside the ads.

## SaaS and B2B

- Optimize for the event closest to revenue that can be fed at volume: demo booked, trial started, or ideally activation or paid conversion sent through CAPI. Start shallower when volume is thin and move deeper as it grows (GrowthSpree).
- Meta suits product-led, broad-market tools. It is weaker where a buying committee must approve before anyone can try the product; say so if the brand fits that profile.
- Consolidate even more aggressively than ecommerce; SaaS accounts rarely have the volume to feed more than one ad set.
- Creative: product demo, founder-led explanation, before/after workflow, customer testimonial, text-led problem statements.

## Local services and lead generation

- Lead forms with Higher Intent and qualifying questions, or website leads optimized through the Sales objective.
- CRM stage feedback through CAPI once volume allows (see playbook section 6 thresholds).
- Location as the hard constraint; value rules rather than demographic exclusions.
- Response time is part of the strategy, not an ops footnote.

## Small markets and low budgets (including Sri Lanka)

- Under about $50/day: one campaign, one ad set, and an optimization event reachable 25 to 50 times a week even if higher-funnel [Loomer].
- Breadth is disputed. Brandlocker argues that around $20/day a country-wide audience starves learning and recommends concentrating geography, fewer concepts and catalog ads; Loomer's default is broad. Both agree on consolidating spend.
- **Domestic Sri Lankan costs** are low but poorly benchmarked and sources conflict. Hype Sri Lanka's 2026 FMCG case reported CPM around LKR 173, with website visits costing far more than clicks because users rarely leave the app. Tectera cites LKR 300 to 1,200 CPMs with no method. Treat both as directional, and for domestic audiences favour in-app conversion paths (lead forms, WhatsApp, Messenger) over website visits.
- **Inbound tourism and diaspora** campaigns pay the CPMs of the source market (UK, Germany, Australia, the Gulf), not Sri Lankan rates. Budget for that and say so.
- No independent source has published hard results for Sri Lankan villas targeting foreign markets. Frame early spend as structured tests and build the plan to generate that evidence.
