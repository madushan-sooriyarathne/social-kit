# Vertical Playbooks

Read the section for the brand's vertical. If none fits, use "Other verticals" and lean on the five winner traits.

## Luxury hospitality (villas, boutique hotels, resorts)

**What the evidence shows**
- Intermediaries are strongest here and now most exposed. Ray's March 2026 data: Tripadvisor, Expedia and Travelocity lost visibility; brand sites won in travel.
- AI answers still lean on OTAs. Cloudbeds study (June 2025; 810 prompts, 145 hotels, 6 destinations, ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini): OTAs supplied 55.3% of citations vs 13.6% for hotel websites. 98% of recommended hotels appeared on YouTube, 97% in travel blogs, 95% on Reddit. Vendor data, **Moderate to Weak**.
- HotelRank.ai vendor data suggests OTA reliance drops sharply for 5-star properties, which favours luxury brands. **Weak.**
- Phocuswright (December 2025): nearly 4 in 10 US travellers used generative AI for trip research in the past year, up 11 points in one year.

**Playbook**
- Make the official site the obvious "official site": strong branded homepage, clear property name and location in titles, consistent NAP everywhere.
- Direct booking on-site with live availability and rates (task completion). Link the booking engine from the Business Profile and hotel metasearch free booking links.
- Server-rendered room or villa pages, each with first-party photography, rate table, amenities, capacity, and LodgingBusiness / Offer schema.
- Proprietary assets: original photo and video, local guides written from real experience, experiences only the property offers.
- Off-site footprint: reviews beyond Google (Tripadvisor, Booking, travel blogs), placement on curated "best villas in [destination]" and "best boutique hotels in [region]" lists, travel press, YouTube walkthroughs, Reddit presence where natural.
- North-star KPI: direct-booking share vs OTA, plus branded search growth.

## Real estate (agencies, developers, agents)

**What the evidence shows**
- Portals and aggregators (Realtor.com) lost ground in March 2026.
- Local Falcon index (August 2026, 37,500 AI searches): 91.5% of agents with websites were never cited by AI. Business Profile was the top citation source (36.6%, about 72% in AI Mode); Zillow second (15.9%). A smaller conflicting study found Business Profile at 57.6% and agent sites at 40.5%. Treat the numbers as unstable. **Weak to Moderate.**
- Indig's ChatGPT data found breadth rewarded in real estate when each topic has depth.

**Playbook**
- Listings and neighbourhood data as the proprietary asset: server-rendered listing pages, area guides with real data (prices, schools, transport), floor plans, original photography.
- Task completion: property search, valuation tools, enquiry and viewing booking.
- A strong Business Profile and a dedicated page per service and per area served.
- Developers: one deep, server-rendered page per project with availability, unit types, pricing bands, progress updates.
- Earn mentions in local press, developer and property directories, and "best agents in [city]" lists.

## Local services (any business serving a defined area)

**What the evidence shows** (Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors 2026, Darren Shaw, 47 experts, published November 2025; expert survey, so opinion rather than correlation)
- Top local pack factors: primary Business Profile category, proximity, keywords in the profile's business title, physical address in the city, being open at the time of search.
- Review recency rose to #11 (from about #93 in 2023).
- For AI visibility: presence on expert-curated "Best Of" lists and a dedicated page per service are top factors; three of the top five AI factors are citation factors.
- Shaw: reviews need to come from more places than Google alone.

**Playbook**
- Get the primary category right, keep hours accurate, build steady review velocity across several platforms.
- One page per service (and per location if multiple), each server-rendered with clear service area.
- Consistent NAP and brand description across directories.
- Target the curated "best [service] in [city]" lists that rank and that AI tools cite.
- SearchPilot: putting the location at the start of the title helped a local healthcare site; worth testing.

## SaaS

**What the evidence shows**
- Bain: B2B software CTR down by as much as 30%; 85% of buyers purchase from their day-one list. Being on the AI shortlist matters more than top-of-funnel blog traffic.
- Shepard's task completion and proprietary asset findings favour free tools, templates, integration directories, documentation and original benchmark data over generic glossary content.
- Ahrefs: recently updated "best X" lists dominate ChatGPT sources for software queries. Earn placement on third-party comparison lists rather than relying on your own.

**Playbook**
- Product-led pages: free tools, calculators, templates, integration pages (one per integration), docs that are public and server-rendered.
- Original data from product usage published as benchmark reports, pitched as digital PR.
- Comparison and alternatives pages that are honest and specific, plus outreach to third-party comparison lists and review platforms (G2, Capterra and category-specific sites).
- Measure pipeline and signups from organic and AI referrals, branded demand, and AI share of voice for category prompts.

## Other verticals

- Ecommerce: retail and apparel brands won in December 2025. Prioritise product pages with proprietary data (specs, real photos, reviews), review schema (SearchPilot +20% on product pages), and category depth.
- Publishers and affiliates: the hardest position. Informational middlemen lost repeatedly from 2023 to 2026. Be candid: the strategy usually needs a business model shift (tools, data, community, services) rather than SEO tactics.
- YMYL (health, finance, legal): heavy volatility in December 2025. Official and institutional sources gained in March 2026. Real credentials and first-party expertise matter, but as a real entity, not badges.
