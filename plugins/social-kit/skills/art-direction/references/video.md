## 1. Asset set: hero film and cut-downs

Plan one shoot that feeds several edits:

| Edit | Length | Home | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero brand film | 60 to 180 s | Website, owned channels, YouTube, presentations | Attention decays more slowly off-feed; room to breathe |
| Feed cut-downs | 6 to 15 s | Reels, TikTok, Shorts, paid social | Hook in 1 to 2 s, brand early and pulsed; long ads in scroll feeds are largely wasted (Nelson-Field) |
| Silent loops | 5 to 10 s | Website hero, listing galleries | No reliance on audio; seamless loop |
| Feature or how-it-works clips | 15 to 45 s | Product and service pages | Information-carrying, one idea per clip |
| Testimonial or founder | 30 to 90 s | About, landing pages | Eye-level, direct gaze, cut with B-roll; avoid monologue-only |

## 2. The hook (first 1 to 2 seconds)

- Open on a person doing something or a striking reveal of place. Surprise concentrates attention (Teixeira et al., 2012); right-brained features up front held attention better in System1 and IPA coding (directional).
- Never open on a logo card or slow fade.
- Write two or three hook options per cut-down so they can be tested.

Hook patterns that fit most brands:
- Reveal: door opens, curtain draws, drone rises to show the view
- Incident: a hand pours, splashes, unwraps, taps; a person reacts
- Contrast: before and after, outside and inside, noise and calm
- Question in picture: something unexplained that the next seconds resolve

## 3. Brand presence

- Get the brand or product visible within the first 2 seconds in feed edits.
- Pulse it: brief, repeated appearances (product in hand, logo on a cup, signage) rather than one long logo block. Same total exposure, 8 to 10% less zapping (Teixeira et al., 2010).
- Distinctive assets (colour, shape, sound mnemonic) register faster for established brands.

## 4. Story structure for brand films

1. Hook (human incident or surprise)
2. Place and character
3. Desire or tension
4. Experience or transformation
5. Resolution with the brand woven through
6. Soft call to action

Follow surprise early with a longer stretch of joy. Favour characters with agency, a sense of place, people interacting and one scene unfolding over abstracted product and voiceover (Wood, System1).

## 5. Camera movement

| Movement | Communicates | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Static, locked-off | Calm, confidence, observation | Luxury interiors, interviews, product |
| Pan or tilt | Revealing space or scale | Views, architecture |
| Push-in (dolly or slider) | Intimacy, significance | Hero reveals, emotional beats |
| Pull-out | Context, reveal of place | Establishing a villa or resort |
| Gimbal walk-through | Guest's-eye journey | Hospitality and real estate tours |
| Handheld | Authenticity, energy | SaaS culture, founder films, events |
| Drone rise or orbit | Scale, location, awe | Resorts, developments, exteriors |

Plan the movement arc to match the story arc: shifts between static and moving, slow and fast, create intensity (Block). Keep one movement per shot and motivate it.

## 6. Pacing and continuity

- Rhythm is a visual component: faster cuts and varied shot sizes build intensity; longer holds suit luxury calm. Feed cuts usually start fast and settle; brand films can breathe.
- 180-degree rule for interviews and dialogue.
- Match cuts on action or shape to link spaces.
- J-cuts and L-cuts for smooth, documentary-feeling transitions.
- Shoot three to five times more B-roll than seems necessary; every A location needs wide, medium, detail and a moving shot.

## 7. Sound and music

- Diegetic sound (water, sizzle, keyboard, footsteps) and on-camera dialogue over wall-to-wall voiceover.
- Melodic music supports emotion (a right-brain feature); choose tracks with a clear melody, licensed for every channel.
- Record clean room tone and ambient beds at each location.

## 8. Captions and sound-off

- Tell the story visually first; sound-off viewing of subtitled video lowers comprehension and enjoyment vs sound on (Szarkowska et al., 2024).
- Add concise captions inside safe zones; captions compete with visuals, so keep them short and off key action.
- Make the audio reward listeners rather than duplicating the captions.

## 9. Aspect ratios and multi-format shooting

- Shoot at the highest resolution, frame for the widest deliverable, protect a centred action-safe core for 4:5 and 9:16 crops. Mark 16:9, 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16 frame lines on the monitor.
- Capture dedicated vertical takes for hero moments (rotated camera or vertical rig); moves, headroom and text space behave differently in vertical.
- Keep faces, product and on-screen text out of the outer areas that interface elements cover; confirm current dimensions by test upload.

## 10. Storyboard and shot list fields for video

Per shot: ID, edit(s) it feeds, tier, purpose, shot size, angle and height, lens, movement and speed, duration to capture, action and talent direction, sound to record, frame-rate (24 or 25 fps for cinematic, 50 or 60 fps for slow motion), aspect version (horizontal, vertical, both), notes.

Storyboard each hero film frame as one line: "F1, 0:00 to 0:02: close-up, hand slides the glass door open, sunlight floods in, sound of waves. Brand visible on the door handle tag [VERIFY]."

## 11. Deliverable specs

- Masters: log or flat profile, ProRes or high-bitrate source, plus graded versions with one master LUT shared with stills.
- Exports per platform aspect ratio with and without burned-in captions; separate music and dialogue stems for re-edits.
- Naming: client_project_date_location_shot_take; usage rights, talent and location releases logged per file.
