# Sector Playbooks

Each list is a default. Discovery wins: buyer questions from reviews and the competitive gap decide what gets added, cut or promoted to A tier. Every shot still needs its purpose, angle, lens, light and variations in the shot list.

## Luxury hospitality (villas, hotels, resorts)

Goal is usually direct bookings over OTAs; imagery must answer "what will my stay feel like and is it as shown". Closest evidence: the Airbnb study (taste-aligned professional photos lifted occupancy about 9%).

Must-have (A tier):
- Twilight exterior with interior lights on (blue hour), and a golden-hour exterior
- Pool at golden hour, empty and with a guest (feet in water, reading on a lounger, face turned to the view)
- Hero room: one-point symmetry on the bed, level verticals, 24 to 35 mm, lamps on, window view balanced
- View from the bed and from the terrace (answers "what do I wake up to")
- Bathroom: wide and a detail (shower, tub, amenities)
- F&B hero dishes plus a table scene with hands serving or sharing
- Arrival sequence: gate or entrance, first reveal of the main space
- Real staff portraits (host, chef, housekeeping) at eye level
- Drone establishing shot showing proximity to beach, town or landscape
- Texture details: linen, stone, timber, local craft

People-in-place: real-looking guests interacting with the space; partial or turned faces so viewers project themselves. Honest space: avoid ultra-wide distortion that over-promises size.

Video: gimbal walk-through from the guest's point of view; hook with the view reveal or a door opening in the first 2 seconds; diegetic sound (water, birds, kitchen); melodic score; cut-downs for each room type and experience.

## Real estate

Goal is qualified enquiries and viewings; imagery must represent space truthfully and answer layout questions.

Conventions (Kelley and practitioner consensus): camera about chest height or lower for interiors, perfectly vertical lines, staging and decluttering as important as lighting, lamps on, window views balanced, twilight exterior as listing hero, drone for lot, context and proximity.

Must-have:
- Front exterior (day and twilight)
- Kitchen, living, primary bedroom, primary bathroom (wides at 16 to 24 mm with shift, plus one 35 mm honest view each)
- Flow shots: one room into the next to explain layout
- Outdoor living, view, garden or balcony
- Neighbourhood context: walkable amenities, transport, schools (map-like drone or street-level)
- Details that justify price: materials, fittings, storage, appliances

Video: gimbal walk-through following a natural visitor route; floor-plan overlay; drone rise for location; feed cut-downs open on the strongest feature (view, kitchen island, pool).

## SaaS and tech

Goal is trials, demos or pipeline; imagery must show real people getting a real outcome with the product.

Must-have:
- Over-the-shoulder shots of real users with the screen readable (or a clean screen-replace plate)
- Hands on devices (brand-selfie perspective)
- Founder and team portraits at eye level, real employees
- Team and culture moments: whiteboards, pair work, customer calls (handheld energy)
- Customer-in-context: the user's real environment (clinic, shop, warehouse) where the product does its job
- Screen recordings at high resolution with consistent dummy data, zoomed and annotated in the edit
- Abstract product visuals kept structured (design complexity) rather than noisy gradients (clutter)

Colour: restrained palette (utilitarian category). Avoid stock "team high-fiving", which viewers ignore.

Video: founder or customer story, eye-level and direct, cut with product and team B-roll; feature clips of 15 to 45 seconds; avoid monologue-only edits.

## Food and beverage

- Hero dishes: back or side light for texture and steam, 45-degree angle for burgers, drinks and stacked items, overhead for spreads and bowls
- Macro details: crumb, glaze, bubbles, garnish
- Hands serving, pouring, tearing, eating; utensils in frame to suggest use
- Kitchen and maker portraits, ingredients at source
- Space and atmosphere at service time, people-in-place
- Rich colour generally helps (hedonic category)

Video hooks: motion (pour, sizzle, cheese pull, steam); slow motion at 50 or 60 fps; diegetic sound is the hook.

## Fashion, retail and ecommerce

- Product page set: front, back, side, detail, scale or in-hand, on-model, in-context. Product images are scrutinised when they help buyers tell items apart.
- Consistent background, light and crop across the catalogue.
- Campaign: low angles for aspiration, model gaze toward the product for product-focused ads, direct gaze for brand-mood images.
- Hands-in-use versions for ads and product pages (higher purchase intent and CTR than faces).
- Fabric movement and fit in motion for video; try-on and styling clips.

## Professional services

- Real partners and staff: environmental portraits in the actual office, consistent grade and light across all headshots
- Client meeting scenes (over-the-shoulder), process details (documents, models, whiteboards, site visits)
- The place where the work happens (courtroom steps, site, studio) for sense of place
- Avoid handshake and skyline clichés

Video: short explainer from a named expert at eye level, cut with process B-roll; client story where permitted.

## Any other sector

1. Show the real thing, not a metaphor for it.
2. Show it in use by real-looking people.
3. One clear subject per frame.
4. Shoot to the buyer's questions.
5. Design for the channel crops and copy space.
6. Build emotion through character and place.
7. Keep the brand present and pulsed in video.
