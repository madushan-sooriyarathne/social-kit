# Output Template

Deliver one document per brand. It is used by the client, the photographer or DP, the stylist and the editor, so keep each section scannable and practical.

```
# [Brand]: Art Direction Guide ([Shoot type], [date or season])

## Summary
Three to five sentences: the diagnosis of current imagery, the core visual idea, the
top three shots or moves, and what needs verifying before the shoot.

## Assumptions
Scope, days, crew, deliverables, locations, talent and anything assumed because the user
was not available.

## Diagnosis
### Current imagery audit (scorecard below)
### Buyer questions the imagery must answer
### Competitive convention and the gap this brand can own

## Visual system
- Concept line (one sentence)
- Brand adjectives to visual rules (table: adjective, light, palette and grade, lens,
  angle, movement, casting, styling)
- Contrast and affinity plan (which frames carry contrast, which stay in affinity)
- Colour rule for the category
- Moodboard brief: 15 to 30 references grouped by light, composition, casting, styling
  and grade, each described in words with what to take from it and a search phrase;
  plus an anti-board of what to avoid

## Shot list
Grouped by location or product, ordered by schedule.

| ID | Tier | Purpose | Size | Angle and height | Lens | Placement and composition | Light and time | Talent action and gaze | Variations | Crops and copy space | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Video plan
- Asset set (hero film and cut-downs with lengths and homes)
- Hook options per cut-down
- Hero film storyboard (one line per frame with timecode)
- Movement plan, sound and music direction, caption approach
- Video shot list (fields from video.md section 10)

## Schedule and light windows
Sunrise, golden hour, sunset, blue hour for the date and location, and a call-sheet
style timeline that puts A-tier light-dependent shots in their windows.

## Casting, styling and props
Who appears (real staff, guests, customers, models), wardrobe palette, props that signal
use, items to remove or hide.

## On-set direction
Roles and decision split, approval checkpoints, talent direction notes, variation
checklist, the perfect-shot checklist.

## Post-production
Selection criteria, retouching brief and realism ceiling, master grade, delivery specs
per channel, file naming and release log.

## Measurement and tests
Business metric per asset set, two or three A/B tests for the brand's own channels.

## Evidence notes
Short list of the findings the plan leans on, with strength labels, and any conflicts.

## [VERIFY] list
Every brand fact, permission or logistic that needs confirming.
```

## Imagery audit scorecard

Score the current library on each line: Strong, Partial or Missing, with one line of evidence (which page or image).

| Criterion | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Professional quality (light, sharpness, resolution) | Professional, taste-aligned images lift demand (Zhang et al.; Li and Xie) |
| Information-carrying vs decorative | Decorative images are ignored; product and real-people images are studied (NN/g) |
| Real people vs stock | Stock people are ignored (NN/g) |
| Hands-in-use and product interaction | Higher purchase intent and CTR (Hartmann et al.) |
| Gaze directed with purpose | Gaze at product lifts product and brand attention |
| Straight verticals and honest lenses | Credibility for spaces (Kelley, consensus) |
| Consistent grade and palette | Structured design helps, clutter hurts (Pieters et al.) |
| Copy space for layouts | Hero images must carry headlines without fighting the subject |
| Vertical and square assets | Social placements need native framing |
| Video present, hook and brand placement | Attention decays after about 2 s in feeds (Nelson-Field) |

## Notes

- Keep the evidence notes short; the guide is a working document, not an essay.
- Use real brand facts only; mark the rest `[VERIFY]`.
- If the environment offers a doc artifact, use it; otherwise write a markdown file and send it.
