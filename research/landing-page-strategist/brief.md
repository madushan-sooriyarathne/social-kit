# Research Brief: landing-page-strategist

## Original request (verbatim)

> Research on specific workflows of a landing page and conversion design strategist role. This should include building and optimizing landing pages, tracking integration, and A/B testing. The key metrics and deliverables for this should be page load speeds, lead capture rate, and form submissions. Only pay attention to the materials written by third-party experts and ignore everything else.

Original research chat: https://claude.ai/chat/24089651-b1bf-4b97-a7fb-7d405bc84285 (2026-11-08)

## Excluded

Google-authored material, including Google-commissioned studies, and platform-authored best practices (Meta, Google Ads, LinkedIn, HubSpot marketing claims). Tool vendors' data only if flagged. See `SOURCE_POLICY.md`.

## Scope

The full landing page and conversion strategist workflow (research, build, optimize, track, test) for hospitality, real estate, SaaS, local services and ecommerce, judged on page load speed, lead capture rate and form submissions.

## Questions to keep answered

1. Conversion research method: technical, heuristic, analytics and qualitative evidence, and prioritization (PXL).
2. Page structure: message match, above the fold, value proposition clarity, CTAs, proof, page length by awareness level, mobile and accessibility.
3. Forms and lead capture: number and type of fields, single vs multi-step, labels, inline validation, errors, privacy microcopy, spam protection, alternative capture (WhatsApp, calendar, chat), lead quality trade-offs.
4. Page speed: independent evidence linking speed to conversion, which metrics to track (LCP, INP, CLS, TTFB, field vs lab), budgets, common speed killers and fixes for Next.js and headless CMS builds.
5. Tracking: GA4 and GTM event design, dataLayer vs DOM triggers, deduplication, consent mode, cross-domain and booking engines, server-side tagging, CRM lead-quality loop, QA.
6. A/B testing: sample size and power, duration and peeking, SRM, guardrails, client-side vs server-side, realistic win rates, the low-traffic playbook.
7. KPI definitions, formulas and benchmarks, with caveats.
8. Myths, expert disagreements and caveats.

## Fast-moving areas (check first every month)

GA4 and Consent Mode changes reported by independent specialists, new experimentation research, form usability research, page weight and third-party script data (HTTP Archive Web Almanac), landing page benchmark releases.

## Watchlist

CXL and Peep Laja, Wynter, Baymard Institute, Nielsen Norman Group, Ronny Kohavi, Georgi Georgiev (Analytics Toolkit), Evan Miller, Luke Wroblewski, Simo Ahava, Julius Fedorovicius (Analytics Mania), Measureschool, Andy Davies, HTTP Archive.
